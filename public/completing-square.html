Lesson 2

Completing the square

Learn why completing the square works, convert between standard and vertex form, and use the result to analyse a quadratic.

Before starting: You should know how to expand brackets and read vertex form. Previous lesson.

1. Where the square comes from

Last lesson we learned that vertex form, a(x − h)² + k, makes the minimum or maximum easy to read. But most quadratic functions are given as ax² + bx + c. Completing the square is the bridge between these forms.

Start with a familiar identity:

(x + p)² = (x + p)(x + p) = x² + 2px + p²

The middle term is twice the product of x and p. If the middle coefficient is 6, then 2p = 6, so p = 3. The constant required to complete the square is p² = 9. This is why we halve the linear coefficient before squaring it.

For a negative coefficient, the same reasoning gives (x − 3)² = x² − 6x + 9. The middle term changes sign, but the constant is still positive. Notice that the geometric picture above is an explanation of the identity, not a restriction on x.

1. Complete the identity

Complete x² + 10x + □ = (x + p)². Find p and the missing constant.

How to answer: Enter the number p and the missing constant separately.

2. Adding without changing the expression

Suppose we want to rewrite x² + 6x + 5. Adding 9 to complete the square seems to change the expression. To keep its value unchanged, add and subtract the same number.

x² + 6x + 5
= x² + 6x + 9 − 9 + 5
= (x + 3)² − 4

Nothing has been added overall: +9 and −9 cancel. The result is an identity, meaning both forms have the same value at every x. The minimum is −4 at x = −3, because the squared part is never negative.

Example: a negative middle term

Rewrite x² − 10x + 18. Half of −10 is −5 and its square is 25.

x² − 10x + 18
= (x − 5)² − 25 + 18
= (x − 5)² − 7

The vertex is (5, −7), not (−5, −7). The expression inside the brackets vanishes when x = 5.

2. Rewrite in vertex form

Rewrite x² − 8x + 7 in the form (x − h)² + k.

How to answer: Enter h and k in (x − h)² + k, including their signs.

3. When the leading coefficient is not 1

If the leading coefficient is not 1, do not simply halve b. First factor a from the quadratic and linear terms. The expression inside the brackets then has leading coefficient 1.

Example: 2x² + 12x + 11

2(x² + 6x) + 11
= 2[(x + 3)² − 9] + 11
= 2(x + 3)² − 18 + 11
= 2(x + 3)² − 7

The subtraction of 9 happens inside the brackets. Multiplying by 2 changes it to −18. Forgetting this multiplication gives an incorrect constant.

The same method works when a is negative.

Example: −x² + 8x − 9

−(x² − 8x) − 9
= −[(x − 4)² − 16] − 9
= −(x − 4)² + 7

The vertex is (4, 7), a maximum. Factoring a negative number reverses the sign of every term inside the brackets.

In general, for a ≠ 0:

ax² + bx + c = a(x + b/(2a))² + c − b²/(4a)

It is often easier to perform the steps than to memorise this identity.

3. Include the leading coefficient

Rewrite 3x² − 12x + 5 as a(x − h)² + k.

How to answer: Enter the three numbers in a(x − h)² + k.

4. Checking a transformation

You can check a completed square by expanding it back into standard form. Compare all three coefficients; matching a few graph points alone is not the usual algebraic proof.

Example: verify a proposed answer

A student claims 2x² − 4x + 9 = 2(x − 1)² + 9. Expand the right-hand side:

2(x² − 2x + 1) + 9 = 2x² − 4x + 11

The constant differs by 2. The correct vertex form is 2(x − 1)² + 7. The following graph compares the two functions.

5. Using the vertex form

Completing the square gives more than a new way to write a function. It tells us the range, the axis of symmetry, and how to find inputs that give a particular output.

Example: find the minimum and a pair of points

Consider f(x) = x² − 6x + 5. Completing the square gives (x − 3)² − 4. The minimum is −4, and the range is y ≥ −4.

Suppose f(x) = 5. Then (x − 3)² = 9. Since both 3 and −3 have square 9, x − 3 = 3 or −3. Thus x = 6 or x = 0. These points are equally far from the axis x = 3.

5. Find a symmetric point

Write f(x) = 2x² + 12x + 5 in vertex form. One point with output −5 has x = −5. Find the other x-value.

How to answer: Enter a, h, k in vertex form, then the other input x with the specified output.

Summary

Make the coefficient of x² equal to 1 inside brackets, halve the linear coefficient, add and subtract its square, and distribute the leading coefficient. Check by expanding. The next lesson uses the same idea to solve equations.
